## 1. Identity, mission and philosophy

**Álquimia / الكيمياء — The Alchemy of Arabic.** Founded by Hapi, an Egyptian educator and entrepreneur based in Brazil. Global education/community brand rooted in Egypt; Kemet is the related heritage-products brand with a separate offer.

**Mission:** Empower 1,000,000 non-Arabic speakers to speak Egyptian Arabic and live the language through culture, creativity, and community. One million is the long-term ambition, not a current learner count.

**Direct definition:** Álquimia is an academy and community that immerses non-Arabic speakers in Egyptian Arabic and culture through practical, creative learning—helping them communicate, connect, and find their place in the Arabic-speaking world.

**Core experience statement:** Decode the language. Immerse in the culture. Transform through community.

**Philosophy:** Learning Arabic is a living cultural journey connecting language, history, sound, spirituality, art and human experience. “This is not just a school. This is a place of transformation.”

**Arabic as a way of life:** Integrate listening, reading, reflection, creativity and human connection into daily life. Develop poetic expression, wit and a personal voice through stories, metaphor, wordplay and art. Intellectual/spiritual growth is an invitation, not a guaranteed effect on everyone.

## 2. Audience and offer

The community welcomes all proficiency levels from complete beginners to advanced from launch, organized internally by level; placement and grouping details remain to be developed. “Non-Arabic speakers” in the mission describes the target language background, not an exclusion of learners who have already developed Arabic skills. ## 3. Method and community economics

**Immersive · Gamified · Skill-based · Transformative · Edutainment.**

An integrated edutainment community where peers learn and grow together through weekly challenges, cultural activities and interactive live lessons, with educator guidance and meaningful feedback.

“Missions, practice, and play.” “Hear it. Understand it. Create with it. Use it.”

**Create through learning / Create as you learn:** Make learner-created work central: stories, voice recordings, letter art, conversations and reflections. Connect creation to the tree’s flowers and the community showcase.

Connect listening, pronunciation, vocabulary, conversation, reading, writing, calligraphy and self-expression. Use songs, rhythm, cinema, stories, art, quizzes and everyday situations. Activities produce something to hear, say, understand, write or create.

**Diglossia / navigating Arabic varieties:** Teach the relationship between Egyptian Arabic and relevant MSA forms, explaining contexts and overlapping registers while keeping Egyptian Arabic central. Label examples so learners understand what to use with whom and when. Detailed sequencing is still to be designed.

**Community-economics interpretation (working proposal):** Peer participation creates shared practice and feedback; shared resources serve many learners; membership funds teaching, supervision and content. Smaller practice groups preserve meaningful interaction as participation grows. “Shared learning creates shared value. Membership sustains the ecosystem.”

## 4. Tree of Learning

**Approved concept; curriculum still in development.** The tree represents each person's learning journey from seed and roots through continuing growth, connected metaphorically to Arabic word families. The mappings below are illustrative, not a finalized course sequence or assessment framework.

| Element | Learning meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Seed | Curiosity and personal motivation |
| Roots | Sounds, script, meaning and linguistic word families |
| Trunk | Connected skills strengthened through practice |
| Branches | Conversation, culture, art, poetry, spirituality and everyday life |
| Leaves | New words, expressions and experiences |
| Flowers | Personal expression, understanding and meaningful communication |

Daily life nourishes the tree. Growth continues across branches; learners develop interests from a shared foundation.

**Retained learning expectations:** Hapi explicitly wants the earlier 3/6/12-month structure kept as a concise working outline. These targets assume regular participation and the 4–6-hour core commitment; adapt to starting proficiency. Detailed curriculum, tree-stage activities and assessment remain in development.

| Period | Stage | Learning expectations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 months | Taking Root | Introduce yourself for 1–2 minutes with support; use basic daily questions/requests; recognize letters and read familiar words/phrases; connect some word families; establish a habit. |
| 6 months | Branching Out | Hold a 5–7-minute familiar conversation with clarification; tell a simple story; understand a familiar short clip with replay; write a short message; explore cultural/spiritual material with guidance. |
| 1 year | Blossoming | Hold a 10–15-minute familiar-topic conversation; express experiences, opinions and feelings; infer meanings from context/word families; create an Arabic story, video, reflection or song performance; learn more independently. |

These are learning targets, not fluency guarantees or certified proficiency levels. Keep this outline without adding further milestone detail yet.

**Confirmed commitment:** Core study/practice is designed around 4–6 hours weekly. Content and varied/repeated application should enable motivated learners to expand toward 20 hours of self-directed practice weekly. Encourage use beyond the course in daily life. These are workload/design intentions, not measured delivery or guaranteed learning speed.

## 5. Content direction

- **Sound/speaking:** listening, pronunciation, rhythm, songs and conversation.
- **Culture/discovery:** Egyptian daily life, cinema, history and word journeys.
- **Writing/creativity:** script, calligraphy, letter art, poetry and expression.
- **Local nuances and wordplay:** contextual meanings, tone, politeness, idioms, cultural references, humor and puns. Explore these through situated conversations, scenes and songs; progressively develop interpretation and creative use as branches and flowers of the learning tree.
- **Community/practice:** peer exchanges, missions, quizzes and live learning.
- **Spirituality/meaning:** ethical/religious vocabulary, spiritual stories, Sufism and Abrahamic traditions are woven into information, lessons and the learning journey with a balanced approach free of religious bias. Connect language and spiritual/religious concepts without promoting a belief; this is not doctrinal instruction or a Quran course.

**Root-and-meaning reels: maximum 60 seconds.** Start with one root and branch into words, meanings and a living use. Visualize the tree: ك ت ب → كَتَبَ، كِتاب، كاتِب، مَكتبة.

**Word Journeys:** Trace documented Ancient Egyptian contributions to Arabic, via Coptic/other intermediaries where supported, and Arabic contributions to other languages, including Arabic–Iberian connections. Hapi explicitly means Ancient Egyptian origins. Distinguish morphological roots from etymology, and documented origins from disputed claims or sound resemblance.

Earlier episode ideas: oud, alchemy, letter ن. Earlier proposed cadence: one 10–15-minute video and 3–5 shorts weekly; frequency remains provisional.

**Reading the evidence:** US$75 is US$162 (68.4%) below Arab Academy's quarterly US$237 price, and US$222 (74.7%) below three monthly US$99 payments. These are price differences between different offers; Arab Academy includes private attention. Álquimia's community experience is not a substitute for an equal quantity of 1:1 teaching. Conversely, cheaper tutors exist: Walaa's 12-lesson example is R$57 below Álquimia's R$405. A lifetime course can become cheaper over a longer horizon. This sample does not establish “cheapest overall.”

ArabicPod101's pricing page [S7] also advertises lower monthly equivalents tied to 24-month plans (including a temporary sale). They are not three-month prices, so they are excluded from the three-month totals rather than multiplied by three.

**Evidence-based positioning proposal:** “At US$25 a month, Álquimia brings live learning, creative practice, Egyptian culture and an ongoing community together in one membership.” Internal support: a lower entry price than the sampled Arab Academy plans. Emphasize the integrated offer; do not claim rivals teach only a small percentage, that Álquimia produces faster/better results, or a numerical learning ROI without comparative learner data. To demonstrate educational value, track attendance, comparable speaking tasks at entry/3/6/12 months, comprehension, learner-created work and member feedback; report commitment and results transparently.
